## 1. Program Overview

The Athanor Research Foundation supports adventurous, hypothesis-driven
research that has the potential to open new lines of scientific inquiry.
The Frontier Science Grants Program (FSG) funds individual investigators
or small teams pursuing ideas that are too speculative for traditional
federal funding mechanisms but rigorous enough to merit serious investment.

The program is intentionally **discipline-agnostic**. We have funded work
in computational neuroscience, atmospheric chemistry, theoretical computer
science, evolutionary biology, condensed matter physics, and the
foundations of statistics. What unites our portfolio is not a topic but
a posture: a willingness to commit to a falsifiable claim and pursue it
with the methods most likely to refute it.

We expect successful proposals to articulate:

1. A **concrete, testable hypothesis** — not a research area, not a set
   of "questions of interest," not a methodology in search of a problem.
2. A **clear theory of why the hypothesis might be wrong**, and what
   evidence would convince the investigator to abandon it.
3. A **plan of work** that would, if executed faithfully, produce
   evidence sufficient to either confirm or refute the hypothesis within
   the project period.
4. A credible argument for why **the proposing team** is positioned to
   carry out this work — what relevant expertise, infrastructure,
   collaborators, or prior results they bring.

Proposals that read as literature reviews, capability statements, or
catalogs of "potential applications" will not be competitive.

## 2. Eligibility

- **Principal Investigators** must hold a doctoral degree (PhD, MD, ScD,
  or equivalent) and an appointment at an accredited research institution
  (university, independent research institute, or national laboratory).
  Industry researchers may apply through a non-profit fiscal sponsor.
- **Career stage:** Open to investigators at any career stage, from first
  independent appointment through emeritus status. There is no preference
  for early-career applicants and no exclusion of senior investigators.
- **Co-Investigators and Collaborators:** Up to three Co-PIs may be
  named. International collaborators are permitted; ARF funds may not
  flow to non-US institutions but in-kind collaboration is encouraged.
- **Concurrent submissions:** A given PI may submit at most one Frontier
  Science Grant proposal per cycle. PIs with active ARF awards may
  submit a new FSG proposal only after their current award is in its
  final year.

## 3. Budget and Duration

- **Budget cap:** $400,000 in **direct costs** over the project period.
  This is a hard cap; proposals exceeding it will be returned without
  review.
- **Indirect cost rate:** ARF caps indirect costs at **20%** of direct
  costs (modified total direct costs basis). This is firm and will not
  be negotiated upward to match institutional federally negotiated
  rates.
- **Duration:** 24 to 36 months. Most awards are 30 months. No-cost
  extensions of up to 12 months are available with justification.
- **PI salary:** Up to two months of PI summer salary per year (academic
  appointments) or up to 15% effort (calendar-year appointments) is
  allowable. Salary support above this level requires explicit
  justification tied to the technical work.
- **Equipment:** Single-item equipment purchases up to $50,000 are
  allowable. Larger equipment must be cost-shared by the institution.
- **Subawards:** Subawards to collaborating US institutions are allowed
  but must total no more than 30% of the direct cost budget.
- **Disallowed costs:** International travel exceeding $5,000/year,
  conference registration unrelated to project dissemination, publication
  fees for journals charging more than $3,500/article (ARF will pay up
  to $3,500), and lobbying activities of any kind.

## 4. Required Proposal Sections

Submissions must include the following sections in the order listed.
Page limits are enforced; submissions over the limit will be returned.
All pages must use 11-point Times New Roman or Arial, single-spaced,
1-inch margins, with figures and tables included in the page count.

| # | Section | Page Limit |
|---|---------|------------|
| 1 | Project Summary (publicly releasable abstract) | 1 |
| 2 | Specific Aims | 1 |
| 3 | Background and Significance | 3 |
| 4 | Hypothesis and Falsifiability Statement | 1 |
| 5 | Research Plan (methods, timeline, milestones) | 8 |
| 6 | Preliminary Data and Feasibility | 2 |
| 7 | Risk Assessment and Mitigation | 1 |
| 8 | Broader Impacts | 1 |
| 9 | Budget Justification | 2 |
| 10 | Biographical Sketches (PI + Co-PIs) | 3 each |
| 11 | Facilities and Resources | 1 |
| 12 | Data Management and Sharing Plan | 2 |
| 13 | References Cited | no limit |

The **Hypothesis and Falsifiability Statement** is the section that
distinguishes ARF from most funders. State, in a single paragraph, what
specific empirical observation would lead you to abandon the hypothesis
under investigation. Vague answers ("if the data don't support our
model") will be flagged by reviewers; concrete answers ("if the
correlation between X and Y is below 0.3 across our pre-registered
sample") will not.

## 5. Review Criteria

Each proposal is reviewed by three external reviewers and discussed by
a panel of seven program officers. Reviewers score on a 1-9 scale
(1 = exceptional, 9 = poor) along five criteria:

1. **Scientific Significance (25%).** If the hypothesis turned out to
   be true (or false), would it change how the field thinks about its
   subject matter? Would it open new lines of inquiry or close stale
   ones? Trivial confirmations of widely accepted facts are not
   competitive; neither are heroic claims with no path to falsification.

2. **Methodological Rigor (25%).** Are the methods proposed actually
   capable of distinguishing the hypothesis from its alternatives? Has
   the investigator considered confounders, statistical power, and the
   conditions under which their measurements would be invalid?

3. **Falsifiability (15%).** Is the hypothesis stated in a form that
   could in principle be refuted by the proposed work? Is the bar for
   refutation set in advance, or does it look like it would be defined
   post hoc?

4. **Investigator Qualifications and Resources (20%).** Does the team
   have the expertise, equipment, and institutional support to execute
   the proposed work? Where there are gaps, are they bridged by named
   collaborators with documented commitment?

5. **Broader Impacts (15%).** Does the work advance the scientific
   community, train new researchers, or produce reusable artifacts
   (data, code, materials) that benefit others?

Proposals scoring in the top 15% are recommended for funding. The
program is highly competitive; the historical success rate is 12%.

## 6. Submission Process

All submissions are made through the Athanor Research Foundation grants
portal at `grants.athanorresearch.example`. (Reminder: this is a mock

- Completed FSG-2026 cover sheet (downloadable from the portal)

  office
- Letters of support from any named external collaborators (not
  counted against page limits)
- Conflict of interest disclosure listing all co-authors, mentors,
  mentees, and close collaborators from the past four years

Late submissions are not accepted under any circumstances. Portal
issues must be reported at least 24 hours before the deadline; ARF
will not extend deadlines for last-minute technical problems.

## 7. Award Conditions

Awarded projects are subject to the following conditions:

- **Annual progress reports** are due each year on the anniversary of
  the project start date. Reports must include a summary of work
  completed, deviations from the original plan with justification, and
  preliminary data or results.
- **Open data requirement:** All non-sensitive data generated by ARF
  funding must be deposited in a public repository within 12 months of
  generation, or upon publication of associated findings, whichever
  comes first.
- **Pre-registration encouragement:** PIs are strongly encouraged but
  not required to pre-register hypotheses and analysis plans on the
  Open Science Framework or equivalent platform.
- **Acknowledgement:** Publications resulting from ARF funding must
  acknowledge the program: "This work was supported by the Athanor
  Research Foundation under grant FSG-2026-XXXXX."
- **Negative results disclosure:** PIs are required to publicly report
  negative or null findings within 18 months of project completion,
  via a peer-reviewed journal, preprint, or ARF-hosted findings
  registry.
